Advanced Solar Tech — Site Improvement Plan

A practical, low-risk roadmap to turn the site into a lead-generating machine for SEO and AI search (AIO) — starting in Victoria and built to scale across Australia. Each phase lists what to do, why, and what could go wrong.

Guiding principles

Before any task, five rules keep this safe and effective — most sites lose traffic not from doing nothing, but from making changes carelessly.

1

Fix the foundation before scaling. A broken form and missing SEO basics must be solved before spending on traffic or national expansion — otherwise you pay to send visitors to a leaky bucket.

2

Never break existing SEO equity. Any page removed, renamed or merged must be 301-redirected. Changing URLs without redirects is the most common way sites suddenly lose rankings.

3

Change in stages and measure. Do one workstream at a time on a staging copy, then measure before/after. This makes it clear what worked and keeps risk contained.

4

Match reach to real capacity. Only target locations you can genuinely service (directly or via accredited partners). Ranking where you can't install creates wasted leads and bad reviews.

5

Keep facts accurate and current. Rebates, prices and figures change — anything published (especially per-state) needs an owner and a review date.

The roadmap

Phase 0 · Before touching anything — safeguards

Set up a safety net & baseline

Make it impossible to lose work or rankings, and record where we're starting from.

Actions

  • Full backup of the site + database (and a way to restore it).
  • Create a staging (test) copy so changes are tried off the live site first.
  • Connect & verify Google Search Console and GA4; add basic call tracking.
  • Record baseline: current traffic, keyword rankings, leads/month, and a full inventory of all 69 pages and every form.

Watch-outs

  • No backup = catastrophe. Never edit the live site without a tested restore point.
  • No baseline = no proof. If we don't measure now, we can't show what the work achieved (or catch a drop early).
  • Editing directly on live (as happened with the file uploads) risks visible breakage — use staging.
Phase 1 · Week 1–2 — fix & foundation

Stop losing leads and switch on SEO basics

Highest return for least effort. Nothing here is risky if done on staging first.

Actions

  • Repair the broken quote form; test a real submission and confirm the email arrives.
  • Shorten the first form step to 4–6 fields (collect technical detail later).
  • Write unique page titles + meta descriptions with location keywords (in Yoast).
  • Add image alt text; standardise the phone/NAP; add click-to-call on mobile.
  • Delete the default /sample-page/ and test pages; fix menu spelling.

Watch-outs

  • Deleting pages without redirects. Any removed page that has links or rankings needs a 301 redirect to a sensible replacement.
  • Fixing the form may reset its connection to your email/CRM — re-test delivery, don't assume.
  • Yoast title changes can clash with the theme's own titles — check for double titles.
  • Shorter form must still record everything you need to actually quote — agree the minimum fields first.
Phase 2 · Week 3–6 — structure, trust & design

Clean up the site and build credibility

Consolidate the sprawl, add trust, and roll out the refreshed consistent design.

Actions

  • Consolidate duplicate brand pages (e.g. solaredge/-2/-3) into one strong page each, with 301s.
  • Build dedicated, richer service pages (solar, battery, EV, commercial) targeting local terms.
  • Add structured data: LocalBusiness, Service, FAQPage, Review/AggregateRating.
  • Create / optimise the Google Business Profile; add real reviews, CEC badges, project photos.
  • Declutter navigation around a single "Get a Quote" goal.
  • Roll out the refreshed, consistent design (short homepage) — preserving URLs and content.

Watch-outs

  • Page consolidation is the riskiest SEO task. Merge/redirect carefully or you can lose existing rankings; keep a redirect map.
  • Schema mistakes trigger Google warnings — validate every type before publishing.
  • Don't remove Shop/Cart/Login if they're actually used — confirm first (are you really selling online?).
  • A design migration can accidentally drop content or change URLs — treat it as a controlled migration, not a rebuild.
  • Only mark up real reviews as schema; fake review markup is a penalty risk.
Phase 3 · Month 2+ — content & AI-search (AIO)

Earn traffic and get recommended by AI

Give search engines and AI assistants genuinely useful, quotable, factual content.

Actions

  • Publish buyer-question articles (cost, rebates, battery ROI, "is solar worth it in [state]").
  • Add FAQ sections with FAQPage schema (concise, factual answers AI can quote).
  • State key facts plainly (service areas, accreditations, brands, warranties) for entity clarity.
  • Add an llms.txt; keep AI crawlers (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended) allowed.
  • Get listed/consistent on the sources AI trusts (Google Business, review platforms, directories).

Watch-outs

  • Thin or mass AI-generated content can be penalised. Fewer, genuinely useful pages beat many shallow ones.
  • Rebate/price figures date quickly — publish with a "last updated" date and a review owner, or you'll mislead customers and AI.
  • Inconsistent business facts across the web confuse AI — keep NAP and claims identical everywhere.
  • AIO visibility is newer and less predictable than SEO — treat it as a compounding bonus, not a guaranteed channel.
Phase 4 · Later — the instant roof-quote calculator

Turn high-intent visitors into instant leads

Planned now, built later. Visitor enters an address → we estimate roof/solar potential from Google → apply your price list → show a ballpark and capture the lead. (You'll provide the price list + calculator logic.)

What it will need

  • Google Maps Platform account + Solar API key (returns roof size, angle, solar potential + aerial image).
  • Your itemised price list and the pricing rules you use to quote a job.
  • A short lead-capture step to send the full quote.

Watch-outs (important — plan for these before building)

  • API cost & quotas. Solar API is billed per lookup; a public tool can rack up cost or be abused — needs rate-limiting and a budget cap.
  • Coverage gaps. Google's Solar data doesn't cover every Australian address (especially regional/rural) — need a graceful fallback (manual estimate) when data is missing.
  • Estimate accuracy = trust & liability. Must be clearly labelled "indicative estimate", not a fixed quote, to avoid disputes.
  • Price-list exposure. A live calculator reveals pricing to competitors — decide how much to show vs. gate behind contact details.
  • Privacy. Collecting address + contact details triggers Australian Privacy Principles — needs a privacy notice and secure handling.
  • Maintenance. Prices, rebates and STC values change — the calculator needs an easy way to update figures.
Phase 5 · Staged — national expansion (all Australia)

Scale from Victoria to the country — carefully

Grow state by state, only where you can actually deliver, using content that's genuinely different per location.

Actions

  • Confirm service capacity first — which states you can install in directly or via accredited partners.
  • Dominate Victoria first (prove the model, reviews, rankings), then expand one state at a time.
  • Build state/city landing pages that are genuinely unique — differentiated by each state's rebates: federal STC + the ~30% federal battery rebate apply nationally, while Victoria (state rebate + interest-free loans), ACT, WA and SA have their own extra schemes, and NSW/QLD/TAS/NT are largely federal-only. This real difference is what makes location pages valuable rather than duplicate.
  • Set up a Google Business Profile only where you have a genuine presence; build local citations and reviews per region.

Watch-outs (this phase carries the most risk)

  • Ranking where you can't service is the big trap. It produces wasted leads, refunds and 1-star reviews that damage the brand — expand only as fast as delivery allows.
  • Thin/duplicated location pages get penalised. Copy-pasting a page per suburb with the name swapped is a known Google target — each must carry real local value (rebates, projects, pricing).
  • Fake Google Business locations are against the rules and risk suspension — no virtual offices you don't operate from.
  • Reviews and support must scale with coverage, or quality (and rankings) slip.
  • Competition varies by state — some markets are far harder/more expensive to win; plan budget per market.
