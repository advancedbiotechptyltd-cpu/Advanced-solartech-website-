Executive summary

The site has a solid foundation — WordPress with the Yoast SEO plugin already installed, a crawlable structure, a sitemap, and a permissive robots.txt. But the fundamentals aren't switched on: there are no meta descriptions, no local-business structured data, and images have no alt text, so search engines and AI assistants have very little to work with. Most importantly, the "Get a Quote" form is currently broken ("Error: Contact form not found"), which means your single most valuable action — capturing a lead — is failing right now. Fixing that one thing is the highest-return change on the whole site.

Below, issues are grouped by area and tagged Critical High Medium, followed by a step-by-step action plan.

1 · Critical — fix immediately

Critical The quote form is broken

The /get-a-quote/ page shows "Error: Contact form not found", and the homepage contact form errors too. Every visitor who tries to request a quote right now hits a dead end — this is directly losing you leads and sales.

Fix: Re-link or rebuild the form (the referenced form was likely deleted or its ID changed in the form plugin). Test a real submission end-to-end and confirm the enquiry email actually arrives.

Critical The quote form is far too long (40+ fields)

Even once fixed, the form asks for 40+ fields — rebate eligibility, roof material, roof shape, tilt, meter type, photo uploads, bill copies — before a person has any reason to trust you. This level of friction causes the vast majority of visitors to abandon it.

Fix: Cut the first step to 4–6 fields (name, phone, email, suburb/postcode, property type). Collect the technical detail later, after they've made contact. This alone typically multiplies completed enquiries.

2 · SEO issues

High No meta descriptions

Yoast is installed but meta descriptions are empty, so Google writes its own snippet for your listings — hurting click-through from search results.

Fix: Write a unique, benefit-led meta description (≈150 chars) for every key page, with location keywords (e.g. "solar panels in Victoria").

High Weak, keyword-free page titles

Titles like Home - Advanced Solar Tech and SERVICES - Advanced Solar Tech contain no search terms or location. Nobody searches "home" — they search "solar installer Melbourne", "solar battery Victoria".

Fix: Rewrite titles around real search terms + location, e.g. Solar Panel Installation in Victoria | Advanced Solar Tech.

High No Local Business structured data (schema)

There's no LocalBusiness JSON-LD, no address, and no opening hours. This is a major miss for a local installer — it weakens Google Maps / local pack visibility and blocks rich results.

Fix: Add LocalBusiness schema with name, address, phone, hours, service area and accreditations. Create/optimise a Google Business Profile.

High Images have no alt text

Site-wide, images lack descriptive alt attributes — losing image-search traffic and failing accessibility (WCAG) standards.

Fix: Add descriptive alt text to every meaningful image (panels, installs, brands, icons).

Medium Thin & duplicate content

Most pages carry only ~600–800 words, all three services sit on one page, and there are many near-duplicate brand pages (e.g. solaredge, solaredge-2, solaredge-3; huawei/huawei-2). Duplicates cause keyword cannibalisation and waste crawl budget.

Fix: Consolidate duplicate brand pages (redirect extras), and expand each service into its own richer page targeting local keywords.

Medium Default WordPress "sample page" still live

The starter page /sample-page/ is still published and in your sitemap — it looks unfinished and adds indexable junk.

Fix: Delete /sample-page/ and any other placeholder/test pages (e.g. /check-form/).

Medium No blog / no content marketing

The sitemap contains only pages — no articles. There's nothing ranking for the questions buyers actually search ("how much does solar cost in Victoria?", "is a solar battery worth it?"), so you miss a large stream of free, high-intent traffic.

Fix: Start a small, focused article program answering real buyer questions with local specifics.

Medium Inconsistent NAP & small errors

The phone is formatted oddly (0061403657585), there's no consistent address, and there are spelling slips in the menu (e.g. "Residrntal"). Inconsistent name/address/phone hurts local trust and rankings.

Fix: Standardise NAP everywhere (+61 403 657 585), fix spelling, and use a clickable tel: link.

3 · AIO — AI-search readiness

How well ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Perplexity, Gemini and Claude can understand and recommend your business. Today the site gives them very little to work with.

High No structured facts for AI to extract

With no schema, no stated address/service-area, and thin copy, AI engines can't reliably answer "who installs solar in [suburb]?" with your business. AI answers favour sites with clear, factual, well-structured information.

Fix: Add LocalBusiness, Service and FAQPage schema, and state key facts plainly (service areas, accreditations, brands, warranties) as clear sentences.

High No answer-style / FAQ content

AI assistants love concise question-and-answer content they can quote directly. The site has none with proper FAQ markup.

Fix: Add FAQ sections (cost, rebates, install time, warranties, battery ROI) using FAQPage schema.

Medium No llms.txt & undefined AI-crawler policy

There's no llms.txt (an emerging file that guides AI models to your key content), and no explicit decision on AI crawlers (GPTBot, PerplexityBot, ClaudeBot, Google-Extended). Your robots.txt currently allows everything — fine if you want AI visibility, which for lead-gen you do.

Fix: Add an llms.txt pointing to your services, service areas and contact page; keep AI crawlers allowed.

4 · Front-end, UX & lead generation

High No instant value for high-intent visitors

Someone ready to buy has no way to get a ballpark price or estimate without committing to a giant form. That's exactly the gap your planned instant roof-quote calculator fills (see next section).

Fix: Offer an instant estimate / short "get my quote" path so hot leads don't bounce.

High Weak trust signals

Little social proof is surfaced: no prominent reviews/testimonials, no accreditation badges (CEC), no real project photos, case studies or physical address. Solar is a high-consideration purchase — trust drives conversion.

Fix: Add real reviews, star ratings, CEC/accreditation badges, before/after install photos, and a clear service area.

Medium E-commerce clutter distracts from the goal

The navigation exposes Shop, Cart, Checkout, Login and Register. For a quote-and-install business this splits attention and confuses visitors about what to do next.

Fix: Make "Get a Quote" the single, obvious primary action; hide or de-emphasise the store/account clutter unless it's genuinely used.

Medium Weak, buried calls-to-action & mobile friction

CTAs aren't prominent within content, there's no persistent/sticky "Get a Quote", and no click-to-call bar on mobile — where most local traffic comes from. The long forms are especially painful on a phone.

Fix: Add a sticky header CTA and a mobile click-to-call bar; repeat a clear CTA after each section.

Medium Presentation & readability

Headings are in ALL CAPS and copy is generic, which reads as dated and less professional. (This is what the redesign we built already addresses.)

Fix: Apply the refreshed, consistent design with sentence-case headings, clear hierarchy and benefit-led copy.
